# Candidate rules

A mechanical [candidate](candidate-registry.md)'s `rule` names a function in the rule registry. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md), the [replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md) and the [parity check](candidate-parity.md) decide through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule`, so a backtest and a forward test of one version run the same code.

A mechanical candidate records the hash of its rule's code, so the YAML version check forces a new version when that code changes ([rule pinning](#rule-pinning)).

## Market state

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState` is a frozen dataclass: what was known about one event's market at its decision time t, from one retail snapshot and the sharp prices its rule needs. The market is the candidate's `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate.market`; a rule decides the markets `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.SPORT_MARKETS` allows the candidate's sport: `1x2` and `totals` for EPL, `h2h` and `totals` for MLB. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.build_market_state` builds it from the event, the market, its quotes (`odds_core.quotes.Quote`, one per stored price row with its snapshot's fields), t, a maximum input age and the rule's sharp inputs (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.SharpInput`), and ignores every quote stamped after t. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.load_market_state` takes the same sharp inputs, loads the quotes with one query, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.rule_quotes`, for the event, stamped at or before t and within the maximum input age of it, and calls the builder.

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.rule_quotes` is the only query behind a rule's inputs. It reads the event's snapshots of the state's market from `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.RETAIL_SOURCES` (the Odds API and OddsPortal) and their back prices of the books in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.RULE_BOOKMAKERS` (the UK retail roster and the Betfair Exchange), filtered in SQL. When the sharp inputs include `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.POLYMARKET`, it also reads the event's Polymarket snapshots of that market: the mid prices (side `mid`) of the book `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.POLYMARKET_BOOKMAKER`. It issues its own query rather than calling `odds_core.quotes.quotes`, so the [rule hash](#rule-pinning) covers it and nothing that other price readers share.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.event_id`, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.sport_key`, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.kickoff`, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.decision_time` | The event (its integer id) and the time of the state |
| `market` | The candidate's market ([market keys](../collection/sport-and-market-keys.md)) |
| `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.snapshot_id`, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.snapshot_time` | The retail snapshot the state was built from, or `None` when it has no inputs (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.has_input` is false) |
| `retail` | Per line, decimal back prices per outcome for each UK retail book (`odds_core.bookmakers.UK_RETAIL_BOOKMAKERS`) in that snapshot |
| `exchange` | Per line, the devigged Betfair Exchange price in that snapshot (`odds_core.exchange_price.ExchangePrice`, with its time, source, time basis and overround); a line without one is absent |
| `polymarket` | Per line, Polymarket's price (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.PolymarketPrice`: its snapshot id, its time and the mids' implied probabilities normalised to sum to 1); empty unless the rule's sharp inputs include Polymarket, and a line whose mids do not price every outcome is absent |

A line (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.Line`) is the goal line of a `totals` price, or `None` in a market without lines (`1x2`, `h2h`), whose state has at most the one line `None`. A selection (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.Selection`) is an outcome on a line.

As-of-t rules:

- **One retail snapshot, co-timed with the exchange when the rule needs it.** The retail input is the latest snapshot at or before t of the state's market from a source in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.RETAIL_SOURCES` whose back prices quote at least one UK retail book and, when the rule's sharp inputs include `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.EXCHANGE`, the Betfair Exchange (`odds_core.exchange_price.EXCHANGE_BOOKMAKER`), picked as a whole by `odds_core.as_of.latest_snapshot`; a tie in time goes to the higher snapshot id. The retail prices and the exchange price an exchange rule compares were therefore taken together (#682). A rule without the exchange input takes any such snapshot that quotes a UK retail book. Live OddsPortal snapshots carry both, and so do live Odds API snapshots that quote the exchange; a snapshot of another market (OddsPortal writes one per market per scrape), one without an exchange price (for a rule that needs it) or one without a UK retail book is skipped for an earlier one. Books absent from the chosen snapshot are absent from the state (#641). Live Betfair snapshots are not a retail source, and FDUK opening snapshots are excluded because their stamp is only an upper bound on when the prices were taken ([CLV target](../modelling/clv-target.md#the-retail-ev-target)).
- **Polymarket.** When the sharp inputs include `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.POLYMARKET`, the input is the latest Polymarket snapshot at or before t of the state's market whose mids price every outcome of at least one line, picked by `odds_core.as_of.latest_snapshot`. It is a separate snapshot from the retail one, with its own time and id.
- **Polymarket time.** A Polymarket snapshot's time is taken as when its price became known. Backfilled history (`odds_lambda.polymarket_odds.resample`, for EPL and NBA alike) stamps each grid point with the latest observation up to `odds_lambda.polymarket_odds.MAX_STALENESS` older than the point, so a historical Polymarket input can be older than the maximum input age implies.
- **Maximum input age.** When a needed snapshot, the retail one or the Polymarket one, is older than the rule's maximum input age at t ([params](#rule-interface)), or there is none, the state has no inputs: no retail and no sharp price. There is no fall back to an earlier snapshot.
- **Half lines only.** Only a line no result can push on counts (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.is_bettable_line`): `None` in a market without lines, and in a market with lines (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.market_state.LINE_MARKETS`, totals and spreads) a line whose remainder modulo 1 is 0.5, such as over/under 2.5 goals. Whole and quarter lines, and a totals price without a line, are dropped from retail, exchange and Polymarket, so no bet can be refunded in part or in full.
- **Retail.** The snapshot's back prices of the books in the UK retail roster, per line. Book keys are canonical, as the writers store them ([Prices and snapshot payloads tables](../storage/price-tables.md#reading-an-envelope)), so the Odds API's `betfair_ex_uk` is the exchange, and books outside the roster are dropped.
- **Exchange.** For each line, the snapshot's own Betfair Exchange backs on that line through `odds_core.exchange_price.exchange_price`, devigged proportionally: the home, draw and away of `1x2`, or the over and under pair of a totals line. A line has no exchange price when the backs do not price every outcome on it or their back overround exceeds `odds_core.exchange_price.MAX_EXCHANGE_OVERROUND` ([ADR-0014](../../docs/adr/0014-ignore-wide-exchange-prices.md)). Retail on a line without an exchange price is not compared with any other line. The close the report scores against is the live Betfair API price, not this one ([ADR-0012](../../docs/adr/0012-retail-ev-against-betfair-exchange-close.md)).

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.input_snapshot_ids` lists the retail snapshot, then each Polymarket snapshot the state read. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions) records it as a decision's inputs, and the parity check replays them.

The state does not match the rows of `odds_analytics.retail_ev.build_event_rows`, which take the exchange price at t from live Betfair snapshots, separately from the retail snapshot, and take FDUK opening snapshots as retail snapshots too ([target functions](../modelling/clv-target-functions.md#retail-ev-rows)). The [retail-EV model rule](#the-retail-ev-model-rule) therefore trains on rows built from market states, not on those rows.

### known_at and recorded inputs

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.market_state.load_market_state` takes an optional `known_at`. When given, it also drops snapshots whose `created_at` is later than `known_at`: the data the system had stored by then. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions) passes its run time and the parity check's data comparison passes the decision's recorded `known_at`, so neither sees a snapshot stamped before t but stored afterwards. The replay backtest passes `None`, because backfilled rows were created long after their snapshot times. Its optional `snapshot_ids` keep only those snapshots: the parity check passes a decision's recorded inputs ([parity check](candidate-parity.md#replay-of-a-decision)).

## Rule interface

A rule is a function `(MarketState, params) -> list[BetIntent]`. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.BetIntent` is a frozen dataclass of `outcome` (`odds_core.market_prices.Outcome`), `bookmaker`, `price_decimal`, `stake` and `line` (`None` in a market without lines); `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.BetIntent.selection` is its outcome and line.

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.RULES` maps each rule name to an `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleSpec`, the function and its params model, or to an `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.ModelRuleSpec` for a [model rule](#model-rules). Each spec declares `sharp_inputs`, the `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.SharpInput` values the rule needs: a decision without each of them has no inputs, and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule` passes them to the state loader. A params model subclasses `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleParams`, which is frozen, rejects unknown fields and requires `minutes_before_kickoff`, a non-empty list of distinct positive integers ([decision times](#decision-times)), and `max_input_age_minutes`, a positive integer: the oldest snapshot the state may be built from (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.max_input_age`). Registered versions use 10 minutes, which the [OddsPortal runs before each decision](../collection/fetch-oddsportal-job.md#runs-before-candidate-decisions) meet. A draft backtest may widen it to screen an idea on older data, and its output names the age it used ([coverage](candidate-backtest.md#coverage)).

## Running a rule

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule` loads the state of an event's market at a time, within the params' maximum input age, as known at a `known_at`, and returns the state and the rule's bets through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.apply_rule`. Its optional `kickoff` replaces the event's kick-off in the state, for a replay of a decision that saw an earlier one. Its optional `bet_selections` are the selections the candidate version already bet on the match at an earlier decision time; the rule's bets on them are dropped ([once per selection](#once-per-selection)). A state without inputs gives no bets, whatever the rule, and the [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions) records such a decision as missed.

## Decision times

A candidate decides each match at every time in its `minutes_before_kickoff` list, for example `[60]` for one decision an hour before kick-off or `[60, 30]` for two. The params model holds the list earliest decision first (largest number of minutes first). `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.decision_times` returns the kick-off minus each entry, earliest first. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions), the [replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md#replay) and the [parity check](candidate-parity.md#replay-of-a-decision) decide each time as its own decision. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.decision_leads` lists every entry of the `minutes_before_kickoff` of a sport's active mechanical candidates whose params resolve, for the [fetch-oddsportal job](../collection/fetch-oddsportal-job.md#runs-before-candidate-decisions) to scrape before each decision.

### Once per selection

A candidate version bets each selection of a match, an outcome on a line, at most once, at the first of its decision times where the rule bets it. A later time may bet a different selection of the same match, such as over 3.5 after over 2.5. In a market without lines a selection is an outcome. The live runner, the replay backtest and the parity check all pass the selections already bet on the match as `bet_selections` to `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.apply_rule`, which drops the rule's bets on them, and decide a match's times earliest first. The live runner and the parity check read a paper trade's selection as its outcome and its recorded line. Repeat bets on one outcome were rejected because they double exposure on a persistent gap and count near-duplicate bets toward a checkpoint. A version's verdict covers its set of decision times, not any one of them ([#656](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/656)).

## Params validation

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.resolve_rule` checks an `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate`: the rule name is in the registry, the candidate's market is one `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.SPORT_MARKETS` allows its sport (and, for a [model rule](#model-rules), in the markets its model is defined for), and the candidate's raw parameters validate against the rule's params model. It returns the rule and its validated params, and raises a `ValueError` naming the candidate otherwise. A draft backtest uses it alone. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.validate_candidate_rules` does that for every mechanical candidate and also checks that the recorded `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate.rule_sha256` is current ([rule pinning](#rule-pinning)). `odds candidates list` and `odds candidates report` call it after loading the registry, so an invalid rule fails there with exit code 1, and a unit test runs it over the `candidates/` directory.

## Rule pinning

A `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate` records `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate.rule_sha256`, 64 lowercase hex characters. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.rule_sha256` computes the current value: the sha256, in a fixed order, of `inspect.getsource` for the rule function, its params model class, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleParams`, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.longest_retail` (the longest retail price of an outcome), `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap_bets` (the gap rules' shared bet picker), `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.BetIntent`, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.apply_rule` (the [once-per-selection](#once-per-selection) filter) and the modules in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.RULE_INPUT_MODULES`, which load, pick and price a state's quotes: `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs` (the query), `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.market_state` (the builder), `odds_core.as_of` (the as-of pick) and `odds_core.exchange_price` (the exchange price). `odds_core.betfair_close`, the close the report scores against, cannot change a mechanical rule's bets and is not hashed for one. Then come the rule's sharp inputs and the values in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.rule_input_constants`: `odds_core.bookmakers.UK_RETAIL_BOOKMAKERS`; the alias map of each source in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.RETAIL_SOURCES` from `odds_core.bookmakers.BOOKMAKER_ALIASES_BY_SOURCE`, by which the writers give price rows their book keys; and the outcomes `odds_core.market_prices.market_outcomes` gives each market in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.RULE_MARKETS`, the markets of `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.SPORT_MARKETS`, which decide whether an exchange price is complete. A [model rule](#model-rules) then adds the source of each module in its `model_modules`; the library versions that fit it are not hashed, and the replay backtest records them.

`odds_core.quotes`, the price reader the report, the health monitor and other readers share, is not hashed, so a change there does not force a new version of every candidate. Code the hashed modules call in turn is not hashed either: the devig and settlement arithmetic in `odds_core.odds_math`, the `odds_core.quotes.Quote` dataclass, the writers' outcome and line matching and price parsing from an envelope (`odds_lambda.price_rows`) and the source of `odds_core.market_prices` (only the outcomes it gives each market are hashed, as values). `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.validate_candidate_rules` fails a candidate whose recorded value differs, saying the rule's code changed and that a new candidate version must record the new hash. Changing the hash in the file is a change to the YAML, so [the version check](candidate-registry.md#version-check) applies to it. `odds candidates rule-hash <rule>` prints the current hash.

## Rules

### The gap rules

Both gap rules take params `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.GapParams`: `threshold` (> 0), `minutes_before_kickoff` (the list of decision times), `max_input_age_minutes` and `stake` (> 0, default 1.0). They differ only in the fair price they compare retail with, and both bet through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap_bets`: for each line of that fair price, and each outcome, it takes the longest decimal price on that line among the retail books (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.longest_retail`; ties go to the alphabetically first book key) and bets that book at that price on that line when `fair[outcome] × price − 1` is greater than `threshold`. It places at most one bet per selection in a decision, and each selection is decided on its own.

- **`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.exchange_gap`.** It serves every rule market. Its sharp input is `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.EXCHANGE`, and its fair price is each line's exchange price. It returns no bets when the state has no exchange price.
- **`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.polymarket_gap`.** It has the sharp input `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.POLYMARKET`, and its fair price is each line's Polymarket price. It returns no bets when the state has no Polymarket price. Its retail snapshot need not carry an exchange price.

## Model rules

An `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.ModelRuleSpec` is a rule whose function also takes a predictor (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.EvPredictor`): the predicted EV of each UK retail book and outcome quoted in a state. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.ModelRuleSpec.bind` fixes a fitted predictor into an ordinary `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleSpec`, so a bound model rule decides through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.apply_rule` like any other rule, with the same once-per-selection filter. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.ModelRuleSpec.markets` names the markets its model is defined for; the retail-EV model's are `1x2` and `h2h`, which have no lines.

Only the [replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md#model-rules) fits and binds a model. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#one-run) skips a model-rule candidate with a `candidate_invalid` alert, and the [parity check](candidate-parity.md#command) refuses one. Pinning a fitted model for a live forward test does not exist yet: it waits on the model passing its re-test ([#657](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/657)).

`model_modules` names the modules that build, tune, fit and query the model, including `odds_core.betfair_close`, which defines the close every training target is measured against. The `odds_lambda` package does not depend on `odds_analytics`, so `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.rule_sha256` imports them by name and hashes their source ([rule pinning](#rule-pinning)).

### The retail-EV model rule

Params `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RetailEvModelParams`: `model` (`retail_ev_price_only`), `threshold` (> 0), `training_matches` and `refit_every_matches` (the [replay's](candidate-backtest.md#model-rules) training-only prefix and refit cadence), `tuning_trials`, `tuning_seed`, `tuning_min_train_matches` and `tuning_step_matches` (the hyperparameter search on that prefix, below; the minimum must leave a training match to validate), `minutes_before_kickoff`, `max_input_age_minutes` and `stake` (> 0, default 1.0). Every count is a positive integer. The tuning settings are params, so the YAML version check covers them.

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.retail_ev_model` reads the state's one line, `None`, and returns no bets when it has no exchange price. For each outcome it takes the longest decimal price among the retail books (ties go to the alphabetically first book key), as [the gap rules](#the-gap-rules) do, and bets that book at that price when the predicted EV of that quote is greater than `threshold`. The model rule and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.exchange_gap` differ only in the EV estimate: the exchange gap's `exchange.fair[outcome] × price − 1` against the model's prediction.

`odds_analytics.candidate_model` holds the model. The rule's `model_modules` are it, `odds_analytics.retail_ev`, `odds_analytics.xgboost_line_movement`, `odds_core.betfair_close`, `odds_analytics.retail_ev_metrics` (the tuning objective), `odds_analytics.training.tuner` (the search-space draws) and `odds_analytics.training.config` (the feature config the model trains under). The installed XGBoost, Optuna and NumPy versions are not hashed; the replay records them in its result instead, so a rerun can match them ([replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md#model-rules)).

- **Model.** `retail_ev_price_only` is the residual arm on the price features of `odds_analytics.retail_ev.RetailEvFeatures`, with no optional groups, fitted by `odds_analytics.candidate_model.fit_model` through `odds_analytics.retail_ev.fit_retail_ev` with the target and loss weights of the [retail-EV target](../modelling/clv-target.md#the-retail-ev-target). XGBoost's `random_state` is `tuning_seed`.
- **Hyperparameters.** `odds_analytics.candidate_model.choose_hyperparameters` runs `tuning_trials` Optuna TPE trials, its sampler seeded with `tuning_seed`, over `odds_analytics.candidate_model.SEARCH_SPACES`: the bounds of the price-only retail-EV experiment's space (`experiments/configs/xgboost_epl_retail_ev_price_only.yaml`), with `gamma` and `reg_alpha` locked at zero in `odds_analytics.candidate_model.FIXED_XGBOOST_PARAMS`. There is no early stopping, because a refit has no validation set to stop on. A trial's score is its mean fold loss-weighted MSE, the objective the [retail-EV evaluation](retail-ev-evaluation.md) was tuned on, over the folds of `odds_analytics.candidate_model.tuning_folds`: expanding walk-forward over the tuning matches (the [training matches that kicked off before the first scored decision](candidate-backtest.md#model-rules)) in kick-off order, each validating `tuning_step_matches` matches after the first `tuning_min_train_matches`, trained on the matches that kicked off at least `odds_analytics.retail_ev.DECISION_WINDOW` before the fold's first validation match, so no training close falls after a validation row. A fold trains on its matches' rows at every horizon, but is scored only on validation rows the rule could decide from (`odds_analytics.candidate_model.at_decision_times`): those whose snapshot is stamped within `max_input_age_minutes` before one of the decision times. A fold without training or validation rows is skipped, and with none left `odds_analytics.candidate_model.choose_hyperparameters` raises `ValueError`. The best trial's hyperparameters then hold for every refit.
- **Inputs.** `odds_analytics.candidate_model.state_inputs` turns a state's quotes into `odds_analytics.retail_ev.RetailEvInput` with `odds_analytics.retail_ev.quote_inputs`, stamped at the state's snapshot time, labelled with its snapshot's source and time basis (those its exchange price carries, read from the same snapshot), with its own exchange price and no Polymarket price or dynamics. A state without an exchange price has none. `odds_analytics.candidate_model.RetailEvPredictor` predicts every input.
- **Training rows.** `odds_analytics.candidate_model.training_rows` builds, for each retail snapshot of a match from `odds_analytics.retail_ev.DECISION_WINDOW` to `odds_analytics.retail_ev.DECISION_CUTOFF` before kick-off, the state `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.build_market_state` gives at that snapshot's own time, and takes its inputs against the match's close (`odds_core.betfair_close.event_close`) when that is the live Betfair close. `odds_analytics.candidate_model.ModelReplay` loads a match's quotes in that window with `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.rule_quotes` and its close quotes with `odds_core.betfair_close.close_quotes`. Only co-timed snapshots give rows, the exchange price is always the row's own snapshot's, and a training row is the input the rule would have predicted from had it decided at that time. A match closed only by FDUK has no rows: FDUK publishes its closing prices days after the match, so a refit at the next matchday would not have the label. A match without a close has none either.
