# Candidate rules

A mechanical [candidate](candidate-registry.md)'s `rule` names a function in the rule registry. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md), the [replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md) and the [parity check](candidate-parity.md) decide through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule`, so a backtest and a forward test of one version run the same code.

A mechanical candidate records the hash of its rule's code, so the YAML version check forces a new version when that code changes ([rule pinning](#rule-pinning)).

## Market state

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState` is a frozen dataclass: what was known about one event's market at its decision time t, from one retail snapshot and the sharp prices its rule needs, the same for each extra market the rule declares ([extra markets](#extra-markets)), and the same as of each lookback point it declares ([lookback points](#lookback-points)). It also carries the fixture context the rule declares ([fixture context](#fixture-context)). The market is the candidate's `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate.market`; a rule decides the markets `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.registry.SPORT_MARKETS` allows each of the candidate's leagues: `1x2` and `totals` for each football league (EPL, the Championship, La Liga, Serie A, the Bundesliga and Ligue 1), `h2h` and `totals` for MLB and `h2h` for NBA; every `odds_core.sports.SportKey` has an entry ([add a sport](../collection/howto-add-sport.md)). `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.build_market_state` builds it from the event, the market, its quotes (`odds_core.quotes.Quote`, one per stored price row with its snapshot's fields), t, a maximum input age, the rule's sharp inputs (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.SharpInput`) and its extra markets, and ignores every quote stamped after t. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.load_market_state` takes the same sharp inputs and extra markets, loads the quotes with one query, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.rule_quotes`, for the event, stamped at or before t and within the maximum input age of it, and calls the builder.

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.rule_quotes` is the only query behind a rule's inputs. It reads the event's snapshots of the state's market, and of each extra market, from `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.RETAIL_SOURCES` (the Odds API and OddsPortal) and their back prices of the books in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.RULE_BOOKMAKERS` (the UK retail roster and the Betfair Exchange), filtered in SQL. When the sharp inputs include `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.POLYMARKET`, it also reads the event's Polymarket snapshots of that market: the mid prices (side `mid`) of the book `odds_core.bookmakers.POLYMARKET_BOOKMAKER`, for the state's market only. Each quote carries its snapshot's `payload_id` (`odds_core.quotes.Quote`), the source run. It issues its own query rather than calling `odds_core.quotes.quotes`, so a change to the price reader other readers share does not change the [rule hash](#rule-pinning).

Every mapping of a state is read-only, down to the price of each outcome, the extra markets' and lookback points' states and the fixture context, and the snapshot ids it records are fixed when it is built, so a rule can neither change its inputs nor the provenance a decision records.

A line (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.Line`) is the goal line of a `totals` price, or `None` in a market without lines (`1x2`, `h2h`), whose state has at most the one line `None`. A selection (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.Selection`) is an outcome on a line.

As-of-t rules:

- **One retail snapshot, co-timed with the exchange when the rule needs it.** The retail input is the latest snapshot at or before t of the state's market from a source in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.RETAIL_SOURCES` whose back prices quote at least one UK retail book and, when the rule's sharp inputs include `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.EXCHANGE`, the Betfair Exchange (`odds_core.exchange_price.EXCHANGE_BOOKMAKER`), picked as a whole by `odds_core.as_of.latest_snapshot`; a tie in time goes to the higher snapshot id. The retail prices and the exchange price an exchange rule compares were therefore taken together (#682). A rule without the exchange input takes any such snapshot that quotes a UK retail book. Live OddsPortal snapshots carry both, and so do live Odds API snapshots that quote the exchange; a snapshot of another market (OddsPortal writes one per market per scrape), one without an exchange price (for a rule that needs it) or one without a UK retail book is skipped for an earlier one. Books absent from the chosen snapshot are absent from the state (#641). Live Betfair snapshots are not a retail source, and FDUK opening snapshots are excluded because their stamp is only an upper bound on when the prices were taken ([CLV target](../modelling/clv-target.md#the-retail-ev-target)).
- **Polymarket.** When the sharp inputs include `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.POLYMARKET`, the input is the latest Polymarket snapshot at or before t of the state's market whose mids price every outcome of at least one line, picked by `odds_core.as_of.latest_snapshot`. It is a separate snapshot from the retail one, with its own time and id.
- **Polymarket time.** A Polymarket snapshot's time is taken as when its price became known. Backfilled history (`odds_lambda.polymarket_odds.resample`, for EPL and NBA alike) stamps each grid point with the latest observation up to `odds_lambda.polymarket_odds.MAX_STALENESS` older than the point, so a historical Polymarket input can be older than the maximum input age implies.
- **Maximum input age.** When a needed snapshot, the retail one or the Polymarket one (or a [lookback point's](#lookback-points)), is older than the rule's maximum input age at t ([params](#rule-interface)), or there is none, the state has no inputs: no retail and no sharp price. There is no fall back to an earlier snapshot.
- **Half lines only.** Only a line no result can push on counts (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.is_bettable_line`): `None` in a market without lines, and in a market with lines (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.market_state.LINE_MARKETS`, totals and spreads) a line whose remainder modulo 1 is 0.5, such as over/under 2.5 goals. Whole and quarter lines, and a totals price without a line, are dropped from retail, exchange and Polymarket, so no bet can be refunded in part or in full.
- **Retail.** The snapshot's back prices of the books in the UK retail roster, per line. Book keys are canonical, as the writers store them ([Prices and snapshot payloads tables](../storage/price-tables.md#reading-an-envelope)), so the Odds API's `betfair_ex_uk` is the exchange, and books outside the roster are dropped.
- **Exchange.** For each line, the snapshot's own Betfair Exchange backs on that line through `odds_core.exchange_price.exchange_price`, devigged proportionally: the home, draw and away of `1x2`, or the over and under pair of a totals line. A line has no exchange price when the backs do not price every outcome on it or their back overround exceeds `odds_core.exchange_price.MAX_EXCHANGE_OVERROUND` ([ADR-0014](../../docs/adr/0014-ignore-wide-exchange-prices.md)). Retail on a line without an exchange price is not compared with any other line. The close the report scores against is the live Betfair API price, not this one ([ADR-0012](../../docs/adr/0012-retail-ev-against-betfair-exchange-close.md)).

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.input_snapshot_ids` lists the retail snapshot, then each Polymarket snapshot the state read, then each extra market's input snapshots in market-name order, then each lookback point's input snapshots from the nearest point to the furthest. The events a [fixture context](#fixture-context) read are `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.input_event_ids`, a list of their own. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions) records both as a decision's inputs, and the parity check replays them.

The state does not match the rows of `odds_analytics.retail_ev.build_event_rows`, which take the exchange price at t from live Betfair snapshots, separately from the retail snapshot, and take FDUK opening snapshots as retail snapshots too ([target functions](../modelling/clv-target-functions.md#retail-ev-rows)). The [retail-EV model rule](#the-retail-ev-model-rule) therefore trains on rows built from market states, not on those rows.

### Extra markets

A rule may read a second market beside the candidate's own, such as an `1x2` rule that also reads `totals`. The candidate's `market` stays the one it bets on; an extra market is a read-only input, in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.also`, and every `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.BetIntent` is on the candidate's market. The rule declares the markets in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleSpec.also_markets`, beside its `sharp_inputs` (a [model rule](#model-rules)'s spec has the same field). `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.registry.resolve_rule` rejects a candidate whose rule declares its own market or one any of its leagues does not decide (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.registry.SPORT_MARKETS`), and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning.rule_sha256` hashes the declaration.

- **State.** Each extra market is built by the same selection as the candidate's own: the latest snapshot of the market at or before t from a `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.RETAIL_SOURCES` source that quotes a UK retail book and, when the rule needs `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.EXCHANGE`, the Betfair Exchange. An extra market never reads Polymarket, which is read for the candidate's market only. Its state does not itself have extra markets.
- **Same source run.** The extra snapshot comes from the same run as the candidate's retail snapshot: the same `odds_snapshots.payload_id` where the writer stored one, so OddsPortal's markets of one league run pair ([payloads](../storage/price-tables.md#snapshot_payloads)), and the same source otherwise. A snapshot of a later run that lacks the extra market does not pair with an earlier run's.
- **Choosing the pair.** The retail snapshot is the latest one for which every extra market has such an input in its run; a latest snapshot without one is skipped for an earlier snapshot, as one without an exchange price is.
- **Each within its maximum input age.** Both snapshots must be at most `max_input_age_minutes` old at t, each measured on its own stamp. There is no fall back to an earlier one.
- **Missing or stale.** When there is no paired extra input, or the retail snapshot or any extra one is too old, the whole state has no inputs, which is the same outcome as a missing retail input: the rule bets nothing and the [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions) records the decision as missed with reason `no_input`. The [odds-web coverage](../operations/odds-web.md) evaluates the same state.
- **Replay and parity.** The live runner, the [replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md#replay) and the [parity check](candidate-parity.md#replay-of-a-decision) all decide through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule`, so they load extras the same way. A decision records the extra market's snapshot ids in its inputs, and the parity check replays them with the rule's extra markets, so a missing or changed extra input shows as a mismatch.

### Lookback points

A rule may read the market as it stood before t, to condition on movement: how far a price has drifted, or whether a book has repriced since the exchange moved. A candidate declares the points in its params ([rule interface](#rule-interface)), not the rule's spec, because the points are tunable: `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleParams.lookbacks` is a list of `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.LookbackParams`, each with `minutes_before_decision` and its own `max_input_age_minutes`. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.lookback_points` turns them into the `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.Lookback` values the loader takes. A rule that needs them requires them in its own params model. A candidate that declares none has no lookback states, and its decisions, hash inputs and recorded ids are the same as before the field existed.

- **State.** The state at a point is built by the same procedure as the decision's own ([as-of-t rules](#market-state)), as of t less the point's minutes, through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.build_market_state` and `odds_core.as_of.latest_snapshot`: the latest snapshot at or before that point, so a snapshot stamped after the point is never read for it, whatever t is. It carries the same sharp inputs and the same extra markets as the decision, so a rule that declares both gets each extra market at every point, from the same source run as that point's retail snapshot. Its own `lookbacks` is empty.
- **Separate age.** A point's snapshot must be at most the point's `max_input_age_minutes` old at the point. `max_input_age_minutes` stays the decision snapshot's age: a decision may need a 10 minute old snapshot while a 6 hour lookback accepts one an hour old.
- **Missing or stale.** When any point has no input, the whole state has no inputs, the same outcome as a missing retail input: the rule bets nothing and the [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions) records the decision as missed with reason `no_input`. A lookback is therefore a requirement: a match without data that far back is not decided, and older, sparser history misses more often.
- **Read window.** `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.input_window` is how far before t the loader reads: the furthest point plus its age, or `max_input_age_minutes` if that is longer. [`known_at`](#known_at-and-recorded-inputs) filters every point alike.
- **Replay and parity.** The live runner, the [replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md#replay) and the [parity check](candidate-parity.md#replay-of-a-decision) all decide through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule`. A decision records each point's snapshot ids in its inputs and the parity check replays them with the candidate's lookbacks, so a missing or changed lookback input shows as a mismatch. The [odds-web coverage](../operations/odds-web.md) evaluates the same state.

### Fixture context

A rule may read two facts about the fixtures around a match, from the stored [events](../storage/events.md) rather than from prices: each team's previous league result, and how many kick-offs fall near the match's own. The rule declares them in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleSpec.fixture_inputs`, a set of `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.FixtureInput` values (a [model rule](#model-rules)'s spec has the same field), and receives them in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.fixture` (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.FixtureContext`). `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning.rule_sha256` hashes the declaration. It is on the spec, like `also_markets`, because which inputs a rule reads is a fact about its code, never a candidate's tunable choice: the rule function reads `state.fixture` unconditionally, so a candidate cannot switch an input off, and the declaration belongs in the hash beside the code that reads it. What a candidate does tune is a threshold in its rule's params, such as the goal difference or the count a rule requires. A candidate that declares none has an empty context, and its decisions and recorded ids are the same as before the field existed.

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.add_fixture_context` loads the context after the market state, through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.fixture_context.load_fixture_context`, the one loader the live runner, the [replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md#replay) and the [parity check](candidate-parity.md#replay-of-a-decision) share. It reads only the events table, with the match's kick-off as the decision saw it (the `kickoff` of `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule`), and a state without inputs gets no context.

- **Previous result.** `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.FixtureContext.previous` maps the home and the away team (`HOME` and `AWAY` of `odds_core.vocabulary.PriceOutcome`) to its `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.PreviousResult`: the event, its kick-off, and the team's goals for and against, with `goal_difference` and `result` (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.TeamResult`) from the team's side. A team's previous match is its latest earlier match of the event's league in the match's season, whatever its status but cancelled, postponed or unmatched. A season runs from 1 August (1 January for MLB, `odds_core.time.season_start`), so last season's final match is not a previous result.
- **Known at the decision time.** The result counts only when the match is FINAL with both scores and kicked off at least `odds_core.as_of.RESULT_LAG` (3 hours, which covers full time `(inferred)`) before the decision time t. With a `known_at`, as the live runner and the parity check's data comparison pass, it must also have been completed (`odds_core.models.Event.completed_at`, when the database learnt the result) by t and by `known_at`. `completed_at` is the time of the latest FINAL write, not of the first, so a run treats a corrected match as unknown before the correction. A replay passes no `known_at` ([known_at and recorded inputs](#known_at-and-recorded-inputs)) and does not read `completed_at`: historical loads and backfills store results long after their matches, so the bound would make every such previous result unknown. A replay therefore sees a score corrected after the decision as known at full time, and a late correction cannot be told apart from a late load; rewriting `completed_at` instead would lose when the database learnt each result.
- **No previous match.** A team with no earlier match that season has `None`, which the rule handles itself, and the decision is taken: a first match of the season is a fact about the fixture list, not a gap in collection. The context cannot tell it from a season's earlier matches never having been collected, so a rule relies on the events table holding the whole season.
- **Previous result not known.** When a team's previous match exists but its result is not known at t, the match is never replaced by an older one, and the state has no inputs, which `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.missing_fixture` marks. The result is pending when the match kicked off less than `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.fixture_context.RESULT_STORE_LAG` (36 hours) before t, because it kicks off after the decision or the results jobs have not yet run: the [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions) records the decision as missed with reason `fixture_pending`, without an alert. Otherwise the result is overdue, because the daily results jobs ([results jobs](../collection/results-jobs.md)) run at 08:00 UTC with a 26 hour heartbeat and 36 hours leaves them a run with margin `(inferred)`: the decision is missed with reason `fixture_context` and alerted. One threshold serves every sport because every sport's results job runs daily.
- **Concurrent kick-offs.** `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.FixtureContext.concurrent_kickoffs` counts the match itself and every other event of the leagues passed as `fixture_leagues` whose kick-off is within `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.fixture_context.CONCURRENT_WINDOW` (10 minutes) of the match's, the boundary included, whatever its status but cancelled, postponed or unmatched. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.candidate_leagues` gives the leagues of a candidate, every league it bets in (`sports`), so a pooled candidate counts concurrent kick-offs across its leagues; every caller passes it.
- **Kick-off times.** An event keeps no history of its kick-off, so every event's kick-off is its stored one now: the match's own is the decision's recorded kick-off, but a concurrent match that moved after the decision counts at its new time, and one cancelled or postponed since counts as absent `(inferred)`. Events stored after `known_at` are never read by a run. Twin events not yet merged ([event merge](../storage/event-merge.md)) distort the context: a twin of a kick-off in the window counts twice, and a twin of a team's previous match is a match with no result, so the decision is missed until the merge.
- **Recorded values.** The run records the values the decision consumed, each team's previous result (event, kick-off, goals for and against) and the concurrent count, in `odds_core.paper_trade_models.CandidateDecision.input_fixture` (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.FixtureContext.to_record`), and the events they came from in `odds_core.paper_trade_models.CandidateDecision.input_event_ids` (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.MarketState.input_event_ids`). The parity check replays from the recorded values (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.FixtureContext.from_record`) and does not read the events again, as it replays recorded snapshots: events' scores, kick-offs and ids change after a decision (a corrected score, a rescheduled match, a merge into a survivor stored later). It reports a recorded event absent from the database as a mismatch.

### known_at and recorded inputs

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.market_state.load_market_state` takes an optional `known_at`. When given, it also drops snapshots whose `created_at` is later than `known_at`: the data the system had stored by then. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions) passes its run time and the parity check's data comparison passes the decision's recorded `known_at`, so neither sees a snapshot stamped before t but stored afterwards. The replay backtest passes `None`, because backfilled rows were created long after their snapshot times. With lookbacks the same `known_at` applies to every point: a snapshot stored after it is never read, however early it is stamped. Its optional `snapshot_ids` keep only those snapshots: the parity check passes a decision's recorded inputs ([parity check](candidate-parity.md#replay-of-a-decision)).

## Rule interface

A rule is a function `(MarketState, params) -> list[BetIntent]`. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.BetIntent` is a frozen dataclass of `outcome` (`odds_core.market_prices.Outcome`), `bookmaker`, `price_decimal`, `stake` and `line` (`None` in a market without lines); `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.BetIntent.selection` is its outcome and line.

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.registry.RULES` maps each rule name to an `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleSpec`, the function, its params model, its sharp inputs, its extra markets and its fixture inputs, or to an `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.ModelRuleSpec` for a [model rule](#model-rules). Each rule family has its own module, holding its functions, params model and spec, and imports only the framework in `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules`: the params base, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.BetIntent`, the specs, the shared helpers and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule`. The registry imports the rule modules, and no rule module imports it or another rule's module, so adding a rule leaves every other rule's [hash](#rule-pinning) unchanged. Each spec declares `sharp_inputs`, the `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.SharpInput` values the rule needs: a decision without each of them has no inputs, and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule` passes them to the state loader. A params model subclasses `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleParams`, which is frozen, rejects unknown fields and requires `minutes_before_kickoff`, a non-empty list of distinct positive integers ([decision times](#decision-times)), and `max_input_age_minutes`, a positive integer: the oldest snapshot the state may be built from (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.max_input_age`). Its optional `lookbacks` is a list of distinct lookback points, each a positive `minutes_before_decision` and `max_input_age_minutes` ([lookback points](#lookback-points)). Registered versions use 10 minutes, which the [OddsPortal runs before each decision](../collection/fetch-oddsportal-job.md#runs-before-candidate-decisions) meet. A draft backtest may widen it to screen an idea on older data, and its output names the age it used ([coverage](candidate-backtest.md#coverage)).

## Running a rule

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule` loads the state of an event's market at a time, within the params' maximum input age, as known at a `known_at`, and returns the state and the rule's bets through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.apply_rule`. Its optional `kickoff` replaces the event's kick-off in the state, for a replay of a decision that saw an earlier one. Its optional `fixture_leagues` are the leagues its concurrent kick-offs count ([fixture context](#fixture-context)). Its optional `bet_selections` are the selections the candidate version already bet on the match at an earlier decision time; the rule's bets on them are dropped ([once per selection](#once-per-selection)). A state without inputs gives no bets, whatever the rule, and the [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions) records such a decision as missed.

## Decision times

A candidate decides each match at every time in its `minutes_before_kickoff` list, for example `[60]` for one decision an hour before kick-off or `[60, 30]` for two. The params model holds the list earliest decision first (largest number of minutes first). `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.decision_times` returns the kick-off minus each entry, earliest first. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#decisions), the [replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md#replay) and the [parity check](candidate-parity.md#replay-of-a-decision) decide each time as its own decision. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.registry.decision_leads` lists every entry of the `minutes_before_kickoff` of the active mechanical candidates that bet in a sport and whose params resolve, for the [fetch-oddsportal job](../collection/fetch-oddsportal-job.md#runs-before-candidate-decisions) to scrape before each decision.

### Once per selection

A candidate version bets each selection of a match, an outcome on a line, at most once, at the first of its decision times where the rule bets it. A later time may bet a different selection of the same match, such as over 3.5 after over 2.5. In a market without lines a selection is an outcome. The live runner, the replay backtest and the parity check all pass the selections already bet on the match as `bet_selections` to `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.apply_rule`, which drops the rule's bets on them, and decide a match's times earliest first. The live runner and the parity check read a paper trade's selection as its outcome and its recorded line. Repeat bets on one outcome were rejected because they double exposure on a persistent gap and count near-duplicate bets toward a checkpoint. A version's verdict covers its set of decision times, not any one of them ([#656](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/656)).

## Params validation

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.registry.resolve_rule` checks an `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate`: the rule name is in the registry, the candidate's market is one `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.registry.SPORT_MARKETS` allows each of its leagues (and, for a [model rule](#model-rules), in the markets its model is defined for), and the candidate's raw parameters validate against the rule's params model. It returns the rule and its validated params, and raises a `ValueError` naming the candidate otherwise. A draft backtest uses it alone. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning.validate_candidate_rules` does that for every mechanical candidate, refuses a league with no entry for the candidate's market in `odds_core.close.CLOSES` (a `None` entry, such as MLB's, registers, and its bets are reported without EV) ([registry](candidate-registry.md#file-format)), and checks that the recorded `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate.rule_sha256` is current ([rule pinning](#rule-pinning)). `odds candidates list` and `odds candidates report` call it after loading the registry, so an invalid rule fails there with exit code 1, and a unit test runs it over the `candidates/` directory.

## Rule pinning

A `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate` records `odds_core.candidates.MechanicalCandidate.rule_sha256`, 64 lowercase hex characters. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning.rule_sha256` computes the current value with `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.code_sha256`: the sha256 of every first-party module in the rule's import closure, then its sharp inputs, extra markets and fixture inputs. The closure starts from the modules of the rule function, its params model, a [model rule](#model-rules)'s `model_roots` and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning.RULE_ROOTS` (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.run_rule`, through which every decision is made, and `odds_core.bookmakers.BOOKMAKER_ALIASES_BY_SOURCE`, by which the writers give price rows the book keys a state selects). `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.closure` lists it.

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.closure` follows every import statement anywhere in a module, conditional, `TYPE_CHECKING` and function-local ones included, and adds each module's parent packages, so their `__init__` code is hashed too. It follows only first-party modules (the `odds_*` packages) and the roots' own modules, so third-party code is not hashed. A closure module with reflective access to modules, code or namespaces raises `ValueError`, so the hash fails rather than leaving code out, however the access is spelt. Imports are checked against an allowlist, not a denylist. A closure module may not:

- import a module outside the `odds_*` packages unless it is on the allowlist `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.ALLOWED_MODULES` (the modules the hashable rules' closures need), or a name `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.ALLOWED_FROM_IMPORTS` permits from an otherwise refused module. Each imported name, and each attribute chain on one, is resolved on the real module object when the hash is computed. One that is a module outside the `odds_*` packages and the allowlist raises, so `from os import sys` and `os.sys` are refused, as is a first-party module's re-export of one. A name bound to a module may only be used to take an attribute, not passed as a value. Star imports are refused too, since they load the submodules a package's `__all__` names;
- name one of `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.REFLECTIVE_BUILTINS` (such as `__import__`, `eval`, `globals` or `vars`), or a `getattr`, `setattr`, `delattr` or `hasattr` other than in a call with a literal, non-dunder attribute name;
- use any dunder name or attribute outside `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.ALLOWED_DUNDERS` (`__name__` and `__init__`), so `__doc__`, `__dict__`, `__class__`, `__globals__`, `__loader__` and the rest are refused, and a docstring cannot act as a value.

The current closures need three narrow allowances beyond the allowlist. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.DECLARED_DUNDERS` (`__all__`, and SQLAlchemy's `__tablename__` and `__table_args__`) may be assigned, never read. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.ALLOWED_FROM_IMPORTS` allows `from types import MappingProxyType`. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.code_hash.MODULE_DUNDERS` lets `odds_core.config` read `__file__` to locate the project root.

The [retail-EV model rule](#the-retail-ev-model-rule)'s closure reads attributes by computed name and dunders such as `__version__`, so its hash fails closed: `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning.rule_sha256` raises for it, and no candidate of it can register. Its replay backtest still runs as a draft, and reports the hash as `unhashable`.

A lazy loader the closure must follow therefore imports statically, as the `__getattr__` of `odds_analytics.training` does. The hasher itself reads sources through `importlib`, so `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning` holds `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning.rule_sha256` and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning.validate_candidate_rules`, outside the package's `__init__` and every rule's closure.

Each module is hashed by its qualified name and its syntax tree with docstrings removed (a body left empty stays empty, so a docstring-only `__init__` hashes like an empty one), in module-name order. So a comment, a docstring or a formatting change leaves the hash unchanged, and any other edit to a closure module changes it, even one that leaves the rule's bets alone. Modules outside the closure, such as `odds_core.close` (the close the report scores against, which cannot change a mechanical rule's bets), are not hashed for a mechanical rule. The closure of `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap.exchange_gap` and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap.polymarket_gap` is mostly `odds_core` and the framework modules of `odds_lambda.candidate_rules`, with their own module `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap`. It holds neither `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.registry` nor another rule's module, and the package's `__init__`, which every closure holds as a parent package, exports only framework names, so a new rule module and its registry entry change no existing rule's hash; a unit test checks both.

The hash pins code, not data. A rule's bets must come only from code in its closure, its candidate's params (pinned by the [version check](candidate-registry.md#version-check)) and the market state. Reflective access is refused, as above. Reading files, environment variables, settings or pickled data inside rule code is not refused and not covered by the hash, so rule code must not do it. No closure module of the current rules reads one in a way that affects bets: the settings that `odds_core.config` and `odds_core.database` read pick the database, and `odds_core.candidates.candidates_dir` locates the registry.

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.pinning.validate_candidate_rules` fails a candidate whose recorded value differs, saying the rule's code changed and that a new candidate version must record the new hash. Changing the hash in the file is a change to the YAML, so [the version check](candidate-registry.md#version-check) applies to it. A change shown to leave every bet the same is recorded in the [rehash ledger](candidate-registry.md#rehash-ledger) instead and needs no new version. `odds candidates rule-hash <rule>` prints the current hash.

## Rules

A rule that may find several qualifying selections in one decision but bets only one passes each, with its estimated EV, to `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.best_bet`. It keeps the bet of the highest EV; ties go to the lowest line, then the outcome first in `odds_core.market_prices.market_outcomes` order, then the alphabetically first bookmaker key, as the [round-1 rules](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/821#issuecomment-6769611091) fix. The [retail-consensus rules](#the-retail-consensus-rules) bet that way; the gap rules bet every qualifying selection instead.

### The gap rules

Both gap rules take params `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap.GapParams`: `threshold` (> 0), `minutes_before_kickoff` (the list of decision times), `max_input_age_minutes` and `stake` (> 0, default 1.0). They differ only in the fair price they compare retail with, and both bet through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap_bets`: for each line of that fair price, and each outcome, it takes the longest decimal price on that line among the retail books (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.longest_retail`; ties go to the alphabetically first book key) and bets that book at that price on that line, staking `stake`, when `fair[outcome] × price − 1` is greater than `threshold`. It places at most one bet per selection in a decision, and each selection is decided on its own.

- **`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap.exchange_gap`.** It serves every rule market. Its sharp input is `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.EXCHANGE`, and its fair price is each line's exchange price. It returns no bets when the state has no exchange price.
- **`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap.polymarket_gap`.** It has the sharp input `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.POLYMARKET`, and its fair price is each line's Polymarket price. It returns no bets when the state has no Polymarket price. Its retail snapshot need not carry an exchange price.

### The exchange-steam rule

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.exchange_steam.exchange_steam` backs the longest UK retail price on an outcome whose exchange fair probability has risen, while that price still beats the exchange fair price now. It is registered under two names, exchange steam ([#771](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/771)) and team-news lag ([#832](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/832)), the `rule` of `candidates/preregistrations/771-1x2-v1.yaml` and `candidates/preregistrations/832-1x2-v1.yaml`, which share one function, params model and spec (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.exchange_steam.EXCHANGE_STEAM`) and so one [hash](#rule-pinning); each idea is its own family, and its frozen grid in `candidates/preregistrations/` binds to the params model's names. Its sharp input is `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.EXCHANGE`.

Its params model, `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.exchange_steam.ExchangeSteamParams`, adds to the base params `min_rise` (> 0), `gap` (≥ 0) and `lookbacks`, which must hold exactly one [lookback point](#lookback-points). It has no `stake`: every bet is one unit.

For each line of the decision's exchange price that the lookback point's exchange price also has, and each outcome with a retail price, the outcome qualifies when:

- its exchange fair probability now minus its fair probability at the lookback point is at least `min_rise`, and
- with `p` the longest retail price of the outcome (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.longest_retail`), `fair_now × p − 1` is greater than `gap`.

The qualifying outcome's estimated EV is `fair_now × p − 1`, and the rule passes the qualifying bets to `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.best_bet`, so a decision bets at most one outcome. A decision without an exchange price at the decision or at the lookback point bets nothing; a lookback point with no snapshot quoting both the exchange and a UK retail book leaves the state without inputs ([lookback points](#lookback-points)).

### The retail-consensus rules

Three rules of round 1 ([#866](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/866)) read the UK retail books against each other. Each returns at most one bet per decision through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.best_bet`, and each params model takes `stake` (> 0, default 1.0) besides the base params. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_quorum_lag.retail_quorum_lag` and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.subsidised_outcome.subsidised_outcome` read the state's one line, `None`, so they bet only in a market without lines; `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.single_book_outlier.single_book_outlier` reads each line with an exchange price, as [the gap rules](#the-gap-rules) do.

**Retail consensus.** The quorum-lag and subsidised-outcome rules compare a book's price with the consensus of the other books, from `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_consensus`, the one module both import:

- **Book groups.** `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_consensus.BOOK_GROUPS` puts books that share a parent's pricing in one group, so they count once: Paddy Power with Sky Bet, Ladbrokes with Coral, William Hill with 888sport, Betfred, and BetVictor. Only these eight books, the backfill manifests' UK retail books, form the consensus.
- **Group probabilities.** `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_consensus.group_probabilities` devigs each grouped book that prices every outcome proportionally ([devigging](../foundations/devigging.md#what-exists)), and a group's probability of each outcome is the mean over its books that do. A group with no such book is absent.
- **Leave-group-out.** `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_consensus.consensus` compares a book with the groups other than its own (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_consensus.other_groups`): per outcome, the median of their probabilities (the mean of the middle two for an even count), normalised to sum to 1. Fewer than `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_consensus.MIN_OTHER_GROUPS` other groups gives no consensus, and the book is not bet. A book in no group, such as bet365 or Betway, leaves out none: the grids say "the target's whole group", and the backfill's `regions=uk` carries more books than the eight grouped ones `(inferred)`. Betfred never appears in the backfill ([#859](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/859)), so a grouped book has at most three other groups there and needs all of them.

The rules:

- **`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_quorum_lag.retail_quorum_lag`** ([#831](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/831)). Params `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_quorum_lag.RetailQuorumLagParams`: `threshold` (> 0) and `lookbacks` with exactly one [lookback point](#lookback-points). No sharp input. A book's price on an outcome qualifies when it is the same at the decision and at the lookback (a quote missing at either point does not qualify, and the book need not price the other outcomes); at least `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_consensus.MIN_OTHER_GROUPS` groups other than the book's own, each present at both points, raised their probability of the outcome by at least `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_quorum_lag.MIN_GROUP_RISE` over the lookback; and its estimated EV, `q × price − 1` with `q` the consensus at the decision, exceeds `threshold`. The consensus counts every other group present at the decision, including those that did not rise.
- **`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.subsidised_outcome.subsidised_outcome`** ([#834](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/834)). Params `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.subsidised_outcome.SubsidisedOutcomeParams`: `threshold` (> 0) and `min_overround` (> 0). No sharp input. A book's price on an outcome qualifies when the book prices every outcome with an overround (`Σ 1/price − 1`) of at least `min_overround`; its estimated EV, `q × price − 1` against the consensus, exceeds `threshold`; and on each other outcome the book's implied probability `1/price` is at least `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.subsidised_outcome.MIN_OTHER_MARGIN` above `q`.
- **`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.single_book_outlier.single_book_outlier`** ([#833](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/833)). Params `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.single_book_outlier.SingleBookOutlierParams`: `min_ratio` (> 1) and `gap` (≥ 0). Its sharp input is `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.SharpInput.EXCHANGE`, and it returns no bets without an exchange price. On each outcome of a line with one, it takes the longest retail price (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.rules.longest_retail`) and bets that book when the price is at least `min_ratio` times the longest price of every other book on the outcome (at least two books must quote it, and a tie at the top has ratio 1) and its estimated EV, `fair × price − 1` against the exchange fair price, exceeds `gap`. It reads every UK retail book, not only the grouped ones.

## Model rules

An `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.ModelRuleSpec` is a rule whose function also takes a predictor (`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.EvPredictor`): the predicted EV of each UK retail book and outcome quoted in a state. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.ModelRuleSpec.bind` fixes a fitted predictor into an ordinary `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.RuleSpec`, so a bound model rule decides through `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.apply_rule` like any other rule, with the same once-per-selection filter. `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.ModelRuleSpec.markets` names the markets its model is defined for; the retail-EV model's are `1x2` and `h2h`, which have no lines.

Only the [replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md#model-rules) fits and binds a model. The [run-candidates job](run-candidates-job.md#one-run) skips a model-rule candidate with a `candidate_invalid` alert, and the [parity check](candidate-parity.md#command) refuses one. Pinning a fitted model for a live forward test does not exist yet: it waits on the model passing its re-test ([#657](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/657)).

`model_roots` names, as dotted paths, the code that builds, tunes, fits and queries the model. The odds-lambda package does not depend on odds-analytics, so the hash finds those modules' source by name ([rule pinning](#rule-pinning)). Their closure includes `odds_core.close`, which gives the close every training target is measured against, so any code edit there changes the model rule's hash.

### The retail-EV model rule

Params `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_ev_model.RetailEvModelParams`: `model` (`retail_ev_price_only`), `threshold` (> 0), `training_matches` and `refit_every_matches` (the [replay's](candidate-backtest.md#model-rules) training-only prefix and refit cadence), `tuning_trials`, `tuning_seed`, `tuning_min_train_matches` and `tuning_step_matches` (the hyperparameter search on that prefix, below; the minimum must leave a training match to validate), `minutes_before_kickoff`, `max_input_age_minutes` and `stake` (> 0, default 1.0). Every count is a positive integer. The tuning settings are params, so the YAML version check covers them.

`odds_lambda.candidate_rules.retail_ev_model.retail_ev_model` reads the state's one line, `None`, and returns no bets when it has no exchange price. For each outcome it takes the longest decimal price among the retail books (ties go to the alphabetically first book key), as [the gap rules](#the-gap-rules) do, and bets that book at that price when the predicted EV of that quote is greater than `threshold`. The model rule and `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.gap.exchange_gap` differ only in the EV estimate: the exchange gap's `exchange.fair[outcome] × price − 1` against the model's prediction.

`odds_analytics.candidate_model` holds the model. The rule's `model_roots` is `odds_analytics.candidate_model.ModelReplay`, which tunes and refits the model. Its closure includes `odds_analytics.retail_ev`, `odds_analytics.xgboost_line_movement`, `odds_core.close`, `odds_analytics.retail_ev_metrics` (the tuning objective), `odds_analytics.training.tuner` (the search-space draws) and `odds_analytics.training.config` (the feature config the model trains under). The installed XGBoost, Optuna and NumPy versions are not hashed; the replay records them in its result instead, so a rerun can match them ([replay backtest](candidate-backtest.md#model-rules)).

- **Model.** `retail_ev_price_only` is the residual arm on the price features of `odds_analytics.retail_ev.RetailEvFeatures`, with no optional groups, fitted by `odds_analytics.candidate_model.fit_model` through `odds_analytics.retail_ev.fit_retail_ev` with the target and loss weights of the [retail-EV target](../modelling/clv-target.md#the-retail-ev-target). XGBoost's `random_state` is `tuning_seed`.
- **Hyperparameters.** `odds_analytics.candidate_model.choose_hyperparameters` runs `tuning_trials` Optuna TPE trials, its sampler seeded with `tuning_seed`, over `odds_analytics.candidate_model.SEARCH_SPACES`: the bounds of the price-only retail-EV experiment's space (`experiments/configs/xgboost_epl_retail_ev_price_only.yaml`), with `gamma` and `reg_alpha` locked at zero in `odds_analytics.candidate_model.FIXED_XGBOOST_PARAMS`. There is no early stopping, because a refit has no validation set to stop on. A trial's score is its mean fold loss-weighted MSE, the objective of the [price-only model experiment](exp-epl-retail-ev-price-only.md#rerun-with-the-pipeline-residual-arm-and-a-fixed-weight-scale), over the folds of `odds_analytics.candidate_model.tuning_folds`: expanding walk-forward over the tuning matches (the [training matches that kicked off before the first scored decision](candidate-backtest.md#model-rules)) in kick-off order, each validating `tuning_step_matches` matches after the first `tuning_min_train_matches`, trained on the matches that kicked off at least `odds_analytics.retail_ev.DECISION_WINDOW` before the fold's first validation match, so no training close falls after a validation row. A fold trains on its matches' rows at every horizon, but is scored only on validation rows the rule could decide from (`odds_analytics.candidate_model.at_decision_times`): those whose snapshot is stamped within `max_input_age_minutes` before one of the decision times. A fold without training or validation rows is skipped, and with none left `odds_analytics.candidate_model.choose_hyperparameters` raises `ValueError`. The best trial's hyperparameters then hold for every refit.
- **Inputs.** `odds_analytics.candidate_model.state_inputs` turns a state's quotes into `odds_analytics.retail_ev.RetailEvInput` with `odds_analytics.retail_ev.quote_inputs`, stamped at the state's snapshot time, labelled with its snapshot's source and time basis (those its exchange price carries, read from the same snapshot), with its own exchange price and no Polymarket price or dynamics. A state without an exchange price has none. `odds_analytics.candidate_model.RetailEvPredictor` predicts every input.
- **Training rows.** `odds_analytics.candidate_model.training_rows` builds, for each retail snapshot of a match from `odds_analytics.retail_ev.DECISION_WINDOW` to `odds_analytics.retail_ev.DECISION_CUTOFF` before kick-off, the state `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.build_market_state` gives at that snapshot's own time, and takes its inputs against the match's close (`odds_core.close.event_close`, given the event's sport) when that is the live Betfair close. `odds_analytics.candidate_model.ModelReplay` loads a match's quotes in that window with `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.inputs.rule_quotes` and its close quotes with `odds_core.close.close_quotes`. Only co-timed snapshots give rows, the exchange price is always the row's own snapshot's, and a training row is the input the rule would have predicted from had it decided at that time. `odds_analytics.candidate_model.training_rows` requires a live Betfair close, so a match closed only by its fallback has no rows. For EPL that is FDUK, which publishes its closing prices days after the match, so a refit at the next matchday would not have the label; for NBA it is Polymarket, which is excluded because the model trains on the live Betfair close alone. A match without a close has none either.
