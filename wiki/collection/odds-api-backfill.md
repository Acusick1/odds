# Odds API historical backfill

Fills in historical Odds API snapshots (the paid `/historical/odds` endpoint, 10 units per region per market per call; the free tier has no historical endpoints; [odds-api-client](odds-api-client.md#cost-per-call)) and final scores. A replay backtest needs FINAL events plus snapshots before decision time (`odds_analytics.candidate_backtest.final_events` selects the events, and refuses the sealed holdout days; [replay backtest](../evaluation/candidate-backtest.md)). Code: `packages/odds-cli/odds_cli/commands/{discover,backfill}.py` with logic in odds-analytics. The steps are on [howto-backfill-odds-api-history](howto-backfill-odds-api-history.md). It is separate from the EPL scripts ([historical-ingest-scripts](historical-ingest-scripts.md)); neither is scheduled.

## Acquisition manifests

A manifest in `backfills/` fixes what a historical purchase requests, validated by `odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Manifest`. Its probe's findings correct it before its full run ([#787](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/787)); after that, a top-up is a new manifest. One manifest covers each league or sport in scope of the purchase ([#774](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/774)), all requesting the `uk` region, which bills as one region and carries Betfair Exchange but not Pinnacle, each requiring `betfair_ex_uk` on `h2h`, and `h2h` only, since the probes found no `betfair_ex_uk` totals in any sport or season ([#787](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/787)). Which books each request returns, per sport, is on [vendor facts](odds-api-facts.md#coverage):

- `odds-api-epl-2022-26.yaml`: EPL from 1 April 2023, the first date the probes found `betfair_ex_uk` on EPL `h2h` ([#729](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/729)), to the end of 2025-26; its 2022-23 window holds only that season's last months.
- `odds-api-la-liga-2024-26.yaml`, `odds-api-serie-a-2024-26.yaml`, `odds-api-bundesliga-2024-26.yaml`, `odds-api-ligue-1-2024-26.yaml`: each league's 2024-25 and 2025-26.
- `odds-api-championship-2024-26.yaml`: the Championship's 2024-25 and 2025-26 league seasons, which holds no play-off fixtures.
- `odds-api-nba-2024-26.yaml`: NBA 2024-25 and 2025-26, each window starting at opening night so preseason is excluded.
- `odds-api-mlb-2024-26.yaml`: MLB 2024, 2025 and 2026 as calendar years.

The planner prices every stored FINAL event in a window, so the MLB manifest's cost includes the 2026 All-Star Game. `odds backfill plan --manifest M --cost` prints each manifest's cost ([cost and execute a manifest](howto-backfill-odds-api-history.md#cost-and-execute-a-manifest)).

Each manifest holds:

- **Content:** the sport, the seasons as disjoint `[start, end)` kick-off ranges, the markets in the Odds API's keys (`h2h` or `totals`), each once and with no line, since a call stores every line the books return, and either named bookmakers or regions, not both.
- **Coverage bands:** each band (`odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Band`) is a range of leads before kick-off in whole minutes, from `min_lead_minutes` to `max_lead_minutes` inclusive, with a max input age. It promises that a decision at any whole minute in the range finds a snapshot at most the max age old, so a candidate deciding at any of those leads with at least that max input age has inputs, since it reads the latest snapshot in `[t - max age, t]` at decision time `t`, both ends inclusive (a rule that declares an extra market reads one such snapshot of each market, from one run; `odds_lambda.candidate_rules.market_state.load_market_state`). A candidate that declares lookback points also needs a snapshot at each point, within the point's own max input age, so a band serves a lookback only where it covers the point's lead (`decision lead + minutes before the decision`). Bands do not overlap.
- **Snapshot grid:** steps by date, each a snapshot interval from its start until the next step's start. A step's start carries the provider's phase to the second, so the grid's times are the snapshots' own: every manifest has 5-minute snapshots from `2022-09-01T00:00:38Z`, since the probes found each 5-minute snapshot 37 to 42 s past its 5-minute mark, varying by date ([#729](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/729)). The 10-minute snapshots before September 2022 fall at 5, 15, 25 minutes past the hour and so on, with no seconds; no manifest reaches back to them.
- **Probe:** the snapshot times to request before the full run and what each must return: fixtures at least `min_fixture_lead_hours` ahead, the `required_bookmakers` on each market, and an `x-requests-last` of the manifest's `odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Manifest.units_per_call`. Validation rejects a lead shorter than the earliest band's farthest lead plus its max age, required markets the manifest does not request, required books its named bookmakers leave out (a region's books are known only from a response), and a snapshot outside every season or off the grid. `odds backfill probe` makes these calls and checks each response against the manifest ([probe](#probing-a-manifest)).

The bands are a 10-minute max age to KO−2h, 1 hour to KO−1d and 6 hours to KO−7d ([#740](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/740)), each ending at the fixture horizon the probes found ([#787](https://github.com/Acusick1/odds/issues/787)):

| Manifests | Bands | `min_fixture_lead_hours` | Farthest fixture listed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EPL, La Liga, Serie A, Bundesliga, Ligue 1 | to KO−2h, KO−1d, KO−7d | 174 | La Liga 154 to 346 h |
| Championship | to KO−2h, KO−1d, KO−4d | 102 | 125 to 172 h |
| NBA, MLB | to KO−2h, KO−8h | 9 | NBA 604 to 674 min, MLB 584 to 612 min |

The probes confirmed the documented billing, 10 units per market per region, for named bookmakers and for `regions=uk` ([vendor facts](odds-api-facts.md#billing)).

## Planning calls

`odds_analytics.backfill_plan.plan` takes a manifest and fixtures and returns the fewest calls, each a `odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Call` with its time and the fixture bands it serves. One call returns the whole league, so the planner works on decision times, not fixtures: it merges every fixture's bands into stretches of time, each needing the strictest max age of the bands that cover it. Walking forward through decision minutes, at the first one the last call is too old for, it adds a call at the latest grid snapshot at or before that minute (`odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Manifest.floor_to_grid`). A call's time is that snapshot's time, seconds included, and a decision minute reads it only at or after it, so every decision minute has a call at most its max age old and no later than the decision, and no fewer grid calls achieve that. A call serves a fixture's band (`odds_analytics.backfill_plan.targets`) when it lies between the band's farthest decision less its max age and its nearest decision. A max age with no grid snapshot inside it, or a decision before the first snapshot, raises `ValueError`.

`odds_analytics.backfill_plan.load_fixtures` reads the season's FINAL events of the manifest's sport.

A call costs `odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Manifest.units_per_call`: 10 units per market per region, where each region a manifest names counts once and each group of up to 10 named bookmakers counts as one region.

## Commands

- `odds discover games --start D --end D` calls `odds_lambda.data_fetcher.TheOddsAPIClient.get_historical_events` per day (the snapshot at or before 00:00Z) and resolves each listed game to its event and creates a SCHEDULED one only when none matches (`odds_cli.commands.discover`; [Odds API events](../storage/event-matching.md#odds-api-events)). A game listed with a start at or before the requested time is already under way, and the API lists it at its actual start, so it is skipped and counted as "listed under way"; a game listed on several days keeps its latest pre-game listing. An evening game before 00:00Z is therefore stored from the previous day's snapshot, and only when that day is in the window. The default window is yesterday to today. Events already in the database, stored by any source, keep their id, status, scores, teams, MLB `gamePk` and acknowledgements, and their kick-off once it has passed, since the [kick-off authority](../storage/kickoff-authority.md) refuses a start at or before now after the stored kick-off ([events](../storage/events.md#writing-events)). It prints the events inserted, updated and dropped, and commits every 100 games.
- `odds backfill plan --manifest M [--season S ...] [--cost]` plans the manifest's calls for each season (`odds_cli.commands.backfill.plan_backfill`) and makes no API call. It prints each call's time and the number of fixture bands it serves. With `--cost` it prints a table of calls and units per season: one row for all bands, then a row per band with the calls that band adds to all the others, so band rows do not sum to the total.
- `odds backfill probe --manifest M [--dry-run] [--pause S]` requests the manifest's probe snapshots through the acquisition key and judges each response ([probing](#probing-a-manifest)) (`odds_cli.commands.backfill.probe_backfill`). It prints the calls and units, asks for confirmation unless `--dry-run`, exits with an error before the prompt when the acquisition key is not set, and prints a table of checks per snapshot. A dry run lists each call it would make, its request time beside the snapshot it requests, with its units, needs no key and reads no database. It exits non-zero if any check fails, or on a call that errors, printing the error without the table.
- `odds backfill execute --manifest M [--season S ...] [--dry-run] [--no-skip-existing] [--max-units N] [--max-consecutive-gaps N] [--max-consecutive-provider-gaps N] [--pause S]` plans each selected season and runs the calls with `odds_analytics.backfill_executor.BackfillExecutor` (`odds_cli.commands.backfill.execute_backfill`), which stores prices only on events already in the database. It prints the call count, the units that many calls cost (`odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Manifest.units_per_call` each) and the acquisition key's balance (or that it is unknown), asks for confirmation unless `--dry-run`, exits with an error before the prompt when the acquisition key is not set (a dry run needs no key), shows a progress bar and ends with a summary table. A run stopped by a guard prints the reason and the summary so far and exits non-zero. See [the executor](#executing-calls).
- `odds backfill scores --start D --end D [--dry-run]` fills missing scores from `nba_team_game_logs`, and sets FINAL ([event-status-lifecycle](event-status-lifecycle.md)). Home/away comes from the matchup string (`vs.` home, `@` away). The game logs come from `odds nba-stats fetch` ([nba-injuries-and-stats](nba-injuries-and-stats.md)).
- `odds backfill status` prints counts of events with snapshots and the earliest/latest snapshot time, then every recorded [provider gap](#provider-gaps) with its sport, market, planned, returned and next snapshot. It counts all `odds_snapshots`, not only backfilled ones.

## Probing a manifest

`odds_analytics.backfill_executor.BackfillExecutor.probe` requests only the manifest's `probe.snapshots`, one call each at its request time ([fetch](#executing-calls)), and stores what each returns through the same path as a run. `odds_analytics.backfill_checks.check_response` judges each response (`odds_analytics.backfill_checks.ResponseCheck`):

- **Lag.** The returned snapshot is an input to the same whole-minute decisions as the grid snapshot the call requested: it falls within that snapshot's whole minute, or exactly on the next minute. The coverage bands and the skip rule rely on the planned snapshot being the stored one, so any other snapshot fails the probe, and the table prints the lag, the planned time less the returned one.
- **Units.** The billed units (`x-requests-last`) equal `odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Manifest.units_per_call`; a response with no such header fails. A response with no events is billed nothing, which the API does not count against the quota ([vendor facts](odds-api-facts.md#billing)), so it may bill 0 or no header, or the manifest's units.
- **Grid.** The snapshot before the response's is one grid step earlier, the step in force at that earlier snapshot, and the one after is one step later, the step in force at the response's own, each to within 60 s (`odds_analytics.backfill_checks.TIMESTAMP_TOLERANCE`) since the provider's timestamps carry seconds of jitter. The API's `previous_timestamp` and `next_timestamp` give them, and a response without either fails. The table prints the gaps to the second.
- **Lead.** The farthest listed fixture kicks off at least `probe.min_fixture_lead_hours` after the snapshot.
- **Books.** Each `probe.required_bookmakers` book, canonicalised with `odds_core.bookmakers.ODDS_API_BOOKMAKER_KEY_MAP` as the response's keys are (so the legacy `betfair` of early history counts for `betfair_ex_uk`), appears on its market (soccer's `h2h` is `1x2` in the response), and the check counts the events carrying it; none fails. The probe judges every listed event.
- **Horizon.** The table prints, per market and required book, the farthest lead (kick-off less snapshot time) of a listed event carrying it, and the probe fails when any is under the horizon within which a run judges books (`odds_analytics.backfill_checks.book_judged_lead`, [below](#executing-calls)).

A call that errors stops the probe, which logs the failed fetch as a run does.

## Executing calls

`odds_analytics.backfill_executor.BackfillExecutor.execute` runs a plan's calls in order. The request comes from the manifest: its sport, its markets and its bookmakers or regions, whichever it names (`odds_lambda.data_fetcher.TheOddsAPIClient.get_historical_odds` sends only that one). For each call:

- **Skip.** Unless `skip_existing` is off, a call is skipped when a recorded [provider gap](#provider-gaps) covers it, or when any fixture it serves already has a historical `odds_api` snapshot (time basis `source_stamped`) of a requested market (soccer's `h2h` stored as `1x2`) in `(request time - grid step, request time]`, where the snapshot the request returns falls (`odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Manifest.grid_step_at`). One stored fixture is enough because a served fixture or market the response omitted is one the API did not list at that time, and fetching again will not change that, so a resume after a failure pays only for the calls it did not store. A live fetch (`observed`) in the window does not satisfy it. It proves that a call was stored, not which books or lines: an earlier manifest's snapshot with other books satisfies it, so a top-up manifest cannot rely on the skip.
- **Dry run.** A dry run makes no API call and writes nothing; it counts the calls that would be made.
- **Fetch.** One request half a grid step after the call's snapshot time (`odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Manifest.request_time`). The API returns the latest snapshot at or before the requested time, and a snapshot drifts seconds either side of its grid time, so requesting at the grid time itself would return the snapshot one step earlier. The response carries the provider's own snapshot time and the call's cost from `x-requests-last` (`odds_core.api_models.HistoricalOddsResponse`).
- **Store.** Every returned game that attaches to a stored event is stored, whether or not the plan names it, with source `odds_api`, time basis `source_stamped` and the provider's snapshot time, which marks it as historical rather than a live fetch's `observed` ([Writing price rows](../storage/price-tables.md#writing-price-rows)). A game attaches to the stored event of its teams within ±2 h of its listed kick-off (an MLB game to its `gamePk`'s event); the backfill creates no event and moves no kick-off, so a game with no stored event, such as a postponed match listed at its original date, is dropped ([historical listings](../storage/event-matching.md#historical-odds-api-listings)). The games attach in fixture-lock order (`odds_lambda.live_events.lock_order`), whatever order the API lists them in, through a finder built from the response's games, so an MLB call reads the MLB schedule once; a dropped game (`no_event`, or `id_refused` when another event holds its Odds API id) is collected and reported once at the end through `odds_lambda.live_events.alert_dropped_games` (its default `stored="odds"` words the warning).
- **Log.** Each call writes a `odds_core.models.FetchLog` row with no quota: `fetch_logs` rows name a sport, and quota views credit a row's balance to the key that sport spends, which is not the acquisition key. The run's last `x-requests-remaining` is in the summary table. A call's rows and fetch log are committed together. A failed call rolls back and writes a failed fetch log, also without a quota, in its own transaction. The call's cost (`x-requests-last`) is not in `fetch_logs`; it is in the structured log (`units_used`) and summed in the run's result.
- **Rerun.** A call's rows commit together, so a rerun after a failure stores the whole call, and with `skip_existing` off a rerun stores nothing new: a snapshot's key is its event, source, market and time, an identical one is skipped and a differing one is kept and logged as `snapshot_key_conflict` ([storing snapshots](../storage/price-tables.md#writing-price-rows)), never merged into the stored one.
- **Pace.** The run waits `--pause` seconds after each paid call (`pause_seconds`, default 0, as is the probe's). The client the executor builds retries a call the API answers with 429 up to `odds_analytics.backfill_executor.RATE_LIMIT_ATTEMPTS` times with jittered exponential backoff ([retry](odds-api-client.md#retry-and-keys)), so a rate limit slows a run rather than stopping it; a 429 is assumed to bill nothing ([vendor facts](odds-api-facts.md#billing)).

Any exception from a call stops the run after it is reported, since every call is paid. Five guards also stop it, raising `odds_analytics.backfill_executor.BackfillStopped` with the reason and the result so far, from the same `odds_analytics.backfill_checks.ResponseCheck` the probe uses:

- **Units.** A fetched call with events whose billed units differ from `odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Manifest.units_per_call`, or are missing, or an empty one billed neither nothing nor those units. The call's rows are stored first, since it is paid.
- **Snapshot drift.** A fetched call that returns a snapshot other than the planned one, judged as the probe's [lag check](#probing-a-manifest) judges it (`odds_analytics.backfill_checks.ResponseCheck.lag_ok`), that is not a [provider gap](#provider-gaps): one off the grid's phase, one later than planned, or the planned one stamped across a minute boundary. The manifest's grid is then likely wrong, and a decision could read an input older than its band's max age. The reason names the planned and the returned snapshot. The call's rows are stored first, at the provider's time, and a rerun skips the call, so a run stopped here resumes only once the manifest's grid is corrected.
- **Cap.** With `max_units` set (`--max-units`), the run stops before a call that would take the run's billed units past it. A dry run bills nothing, so it never reaches the cap.
- **Sparse streak.** `max_consecutive_gaps` (`--max-consecutive-gaps`, default 10, at least 1) consecutive fetched calls are sparse, so one sparse snapshot does not stop a run. A call is sparse when it returns no events, when none of the fixtures it serves was stored (an unmatched or dropped listing, such as a broken matcher), or when a required book is missing from a market. The books are judged only on the stored fixtures the call serves that kick off within the judged horizon of the snapshot (`odds_analytics.backfill_checks.book_judged_lead`): 24 h, or the probe's `min_fixture_lead_hours` when shorter, as for NBA and MLB, whose games are listed only hours ahead. Books are not all posted days ahead (inferred; the probe's horizon check tests it), and that is all a call lists across a break (an All-Star or international break); a call serving no such fixture is not judged on books. A good call resets the streak and a skipped call leaves it unchanged.
- **Gap streak.** `max_consecutive_provider_gaps` (`--max-consecutive-provider-gaps`, default 5, at least 1) consecutive fetched calls are provider gaps. Holes are rare, so a run of them suggests something systematic, such as a grid the provider does not follow (inferred). It counts only consecutive gaps, so it misses gaps interleaved with good calls, and a skipped call inside a known hole does not count. A call returning the planned snapshot resets the streak and a skipped call leaves it unchanged. It is a separate streak from the sparse one, since a gap with full data is not sparse and a sparse call is not a gap.

### Provider gaps

The provider's history has holes, grid times with no snapshot, where the API returns the latest earlier one ([vendor facts](odds-api-facts.md#snapshots)). A call is a provider gap (`odds_analytics.backfill_checks.ResponseCheck.provider_gap`) when the returned snapshot fails the lag check, is earlier than planned, and lies on the grid to within 60 s (`odds_analytics.backfill_checks.TIMESTAMP_TOLERANCE`) at least one step back. No later purchase can fill a hole, so the run stores the call's rows as usual, at the returned snapshot's time, logs `backfill_provider_gap` with the planned, returned and next snapshot, and continues. The decision minutes the planned snapshot would have served read the older snapshot, or nothing when it is older than their band's max age.

The call records one `odds_core.backfill_models.OddsApiSnapshotGap` row (table `odds_api_snapshot_gaps`) per requested market (soccer's `h2h` as `1x2`), keyed by sport, market and planned snapshot, with the returned snapshot and the response's `next_timestamp`, in the same transaction as its rows. Its stored rows lie before the skip window of `(request time - grid step, request time]`, so the stored-snapshot skip cannot see it; instead a rerun skips a call when a gap of a requested market is recorded for its planned snapshot, or when a gap's returned snapshot is earlier than the call's planned snapshot by more than the 60 s jitter and its next snapshot is later than the call's request time, so the request would return the same earlier snapshot. That second rule also skips, within one run, later calls inside a hole the first call found, relying on `next_timestamp` naming the next snapshot that exists (inferred). The audit of decision minutes reads the gaps from `odds backfill status` or the table. The probe records a gap the same way, though it still fails the lag check.

The result counts calls made, skipped and would-be-made, the provider gaps, the units used (the sum of the responses' `x-requests-last`), the fixtures and snapshots stored, the dropped games (each distinct game once, with the number of calls that listed it, so the summary table and the `games_dropped:odds-backfill` alert count games, not listings) and the last quota remaining (`odds_analytics.backfill_executor.BackfillResult`).

## Limits

- The manifest planner refuses a kick-off between whole minutes (`odds_analytics.backfill_plan.Fixture`), since it steps decisions a whole minute at a time. No stored kick-off has seconds (production, 2026-11-11).
- Every backfill call spends the acquisition key, the key at `odds_core.config.APIConfig.acquisition_key_index` (`ODDS_API_ACQUISITION_KEY_INDEX`, no default), whatever the manifest's sport: `odds_analytics.backfill_executor.BackfillExecutor` builds its client with `odds_core.config.APIConfig.acquisition_key`, which overrides the per-sport allocation that live collection keeps and raises `ValueError` when the index is unset, so a real run never falls back to a live key ([keys](odds-api-client.md#retry-and-keys)).
- Manifests of different sports can run as parallel processes. Fixture advisory locks are keyed by sport and teams (`odds_lambda.storage.event_resolver.event_lock_key`), so runs of different sports never wait on each other's locks, and each run takes its own in ascending order; their other writes, a payload, snapshots of their own events and a fetch log per call, are inserts that do not conflict.
- `basketball_nba` is still literal in `odds backfill scores` (`odds_cli.commands.backfill`, which reads game logs and spends no units). `odds discover games` requires `--sport`. `odds backfill plan` and `odds backfill execute` take the manifest's sport, and `odds backfill plan --cost` and `odds backfill execute` print the acquisition key's balance (the newest quota recorded by live collection for that key index, reported as unknown when none is recent or the key is not set).
- Historical responses for soccer carry the three-way market as `1x2`, relabelled, and bookmaker keys renamed to pipeline keys, by `odds_lambda.data_fetcher.TheOddsAPIClient.get_historical_odds` ([sport-and-market-keys](sport-and-market-keys.md#market-key-dialects)).
- Final scores are not available from the Odds API for games older than 3 days (`odds_cli.commands.discover` docstring), hence `backfill scores`.
- `odds validate` reports which fetch tiers a game's snapshots cover (`odds_lambda.storage.tier_validator.TierCoverageValidator`, whose discovery check asks `/scores` for the sport's games on a day of the league's calendar; [fetch-tier](../storage/fetch-tier.md#the-tier-a-snapshot-covers)).

## Tests

`tests/integration/test_backfill_guards.py` replays recorded responses that pass and fail each probe check and each run guard, the providers' recorded holes as gaps that are stored, recorded and skipped on rerun, and `tests/unit/test_backfill_probe_cli.py` runs a dry-run probe offline. `tests/integration/test_backfill_replay.py` replays a constructed historical response for each manifest, in the API's documented shape, through the real client and executor on the test database, with `uk`-region keys stored under their canonical books (`betfair_ex_uk` and `betfair_sb_uk` apart, an unaliased key as given); `tests/integration/test_backfill_guards.py` also checks that the legacy `betfair` key satisfies a required `betfair_ex_uk`: one test per football league, NBA and MLB, then the rerun cases and the MLB doubleheader and postponed game. `tests/integration/test_historical_fixture_attach.py` covers matching a listing to a stored fixture.
